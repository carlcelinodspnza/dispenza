Service pager — five designs

All five are shown as they would appear on service 05, Conversion rate optimization — previous is 04 Advertisement design, next is 06 Integrations. Every number and title is verbatim from the mega menu. The dashed frame is just the review stage, not part of any design.

What is wrong with the current one. Two heavy bordered cards of equal weight sit either side of a small centred label, so the eye has no idea which one is the action — and the boxes are mostly empty, because a service title is short and the card is half the row. Every option below fixes that by either removing the boxes or making the two ends visibly unequal.

Option 1

Rule — no boxes

A hairline and one row of type. Nothing is boxed, so nothing looks empty; the next end is white with an accent number and caret, the previous is muted. Quietest of the five and the shortest by a wide margin.

Option 2

Next bar — one clear action

Commits to forward motion: the next service is a full-width accent bar with a round go button, and previous is demoted to a small text link above it. Use this if the point of the module is to keep people moving through the eight.

Option 3

Index — an actual page nav

Takes "page nav" literally: all eight are present as numbered keys with the current one filled, chevrons at each end, and the next service named underneath. The only option that lets someone jump straight to service 07 without going back to the menu.

Option 4

Split band — two halves, one divider

Keeps both ends equally reachable but removes the card borders entirely: a single band ruled top and bottom, split down the middle. The halves fill on hover, so the target is the whole half rather than a small box.

Option 5

Ghost card — echoes the mega menu

Reuses the treatment just shipped on the mega-menu preview: the next service is a purple gradient card with its number as a watermark, previous is a quiet outline. The two ends are deliberately unequal in width as well as weight, so the action is unmistakable.
