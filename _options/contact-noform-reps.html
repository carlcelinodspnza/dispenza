Contact without the form — the rep bench

You said you're planning to drop the form and add three reps. Two things came out of checking the build, and both change the shape of that plan.

1 — You don't need to source three new reps. Four already exist in the build. Six team members have live, published bio pages; the contact page only ever listed two of them. Every one of the four missing has a page AND a portrait already in assets/in-pages/. No new people, no new pages, no new photography.
2 — Removing the form with nothing in its place dead-ends the funnel. That form is one of only two on the entire site, and the other one (on dispensary-marketing-and-advertising.html) submits to contact.html, so it feeds the form you'd be deleting. The rep bio pages carry no form and no submit button — they each link back to contact.html six times. And of the 87 demo CTAs across the site, only the header/drawer ones open the booking modal; roughly 82 in-page ones navigate to contact.html. Delete the form and every one of those paths lands on a page with nothing to do.

The replacement is already built and already shipped. demo-modal.js — the 8-step Request a Demo flow — loads on all 27 pages. It only intercepts header and drawer CTAs today, by deliberate choice recorded in its own source comment. Pointing the in-page CTAs and the rep buttons at it turns the modal into the booking mechanism everywhere, which is what makes dropping the inline form safe rather than destructive.

Which three is a judgement call, and it is yours. "Select a rep" implies the person who will run your demo. Nick Marx (Client Relations) fits that squarely and James Compton (CEO) plausibly. Worth picking on purpose rather than by who happens to have a square photo.
3 — The portraits are not one set, and it shows the moment they sit in a row. Sergio, Thomas, James and Nick all share the same treatment: the subject cut out over a purple hexagon arc on near-black. Shay's is a plain light-grey studio headshot and Frances's is an uncropped 4000×2661 landscape. In grid 2 below, Shay's card reads as a bright rectangle against four dark ones — it is the first thing the eye goes to, for the wrong reason. This is the same problem as the case-study banners: it is fixed by recompositing onto the house background, not by CSS. Flagging it as a real art dependency before anything ships.
