Mega menu — four elevated treatments

All eight service names, descriptions, links and numbers are verbatim from index.html, as are the "Our suite" eyebrow and the pricing note. Nothing is invented and nothing here is wired to the live panel.

Why the current panel reads flat. Three things, all measurable in the CSS rather than a matter of taste. The shell is a single opaque fill — --ds-bg-surface-2 (#222429) with a 1px border and no shadow, no gradient and no texture, so it has no relationship to the page behind it. The preview card is a solid block of --ds-accent-deep (#420f7b) with nothing in it but text. And the eight rows are plain text separated by hairlines, with the numbers set in the same muted grey as the descriptions — so nothing in the panel has any hierarchy except font size. Every treatment below fixes at least the first two.

Treatment A

Lit — same bones, real depth

The most conservative: identical structure and row order, but the shell gains a gradient, an inner top highlight and a real drop shadow so it reads as a panel floating over the page; the site's own hexagon field appears at 10% in the top-left; the preview card becomes a gradient with a corner glow; and each row gains an accent spine, an arrow and an accent numeral on hover.

Treatment B

Tiles — every service is a card

Drops the preview column and gives all eight equal weight as tiles, four across. Each gets a gradient number chip, and the whole tile lifts on hover. A promo strip carries the pricing note as a real call to action rather than a line of small print.

Treatment C

Spotlight — one big preview, one clean index

The preview grows and carries a huge ghost numeral; the eight become a single full-width index where the title and description share one line, so the list is shorter and scans faster. Hovering slides the row right and washes it with accent.

Treatment D

Compact — half the height

For the case where the menu is too tall rather than too plain: descriptions drop out, the preview goes, and the eight become a tight two-column index. The pricing note becomes a footer strip. Shortest of the four by a long way.
